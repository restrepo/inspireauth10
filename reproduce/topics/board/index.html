Growing, stable and shrinking topics

A topic's trend compares its share of all papers with a topic in 2021–2025 with its share in 2011–2015, so the growth of the whole literature (×1.32 between the two periods) is taken out: Growing when the share rose by more than half (×1.5), Shrinking when it fell by more than a third (below ×0.67), Stable otherwise. The trend is also in the Topics table and in the data (topics.jsonl.gz).

Where the authors of a topic went

Each author with an INSPIRE profile gets a main topic in a period: the topic with most of their papers, each paper counting 1/number of authors, papers with more than 10 authors left out. Authors with at least 2 papers in the early period are followed to the late period: their main topic there, or stopped when they have no paper with a topic in it (left the field, retired, or now publish only in large collaborations). Profiles cover most but not all authors.

How the topics were made

  • Network. Every paper of the tables is a node, the corpus of 1 to 10 authors and the collaboration papers alike; a link joins two papers when one cites the other (— links). This is the direct-citation approach of Waltman & van Eck (2012), used by CWTS for the OpenAlex topics, but nothing here is mapped to OpenAlex, arXiv or any other classification.
  • Topics. Leiden clustering (Traag, Waltman & van Eck 2019) maximizing modularity at resolution —, with igraph, up to 20 passes. Clusters under 100 papers join the cluster they share the most links with. Papers with no link (old records, theses, notes) get no topic.
  • Seeds. Leiden is randomized. Four runs with different seeds agree at NMI 0.84–0.85 and ARI 0.64–0.69 for topics; the run that agrees best with the others is kept. Small topics shift a little between runs, so their exact borders should not be over-read.
  • Areas. Leiden on the graph of topics, with the links inside each topic kept, at resolution —, the best of 50 runs; the four seeds agree at ARI 0.85–0.90.
  • Names. Each topic was named from the titles of its most cited papers (from INSPIRE), checked against its arXiv categories and journals.
  • Map. Topics are placed by a force layout of the graph of topics, each topic linked to its 6 strongest neighbours by association strength (links between two topics over the product of their total links).
